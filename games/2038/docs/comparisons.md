# Mandate 2038 Comparative References

**Purpose:** positioning and design-reference aid. These comparisons identify
useful player expectations; they do not describe direct mechanical equivalence.

## Short version

**Mandate 2038 is New Angeles × Gaia Project, with Oath-like stakes over what
kind of world results.**

## Closest references

### New Angeles — institutional theme and public/private tension

This is the closest thematic reference: competing corporate institutions,
negotiation, public instability, and private victory are intertwined with a
shared civic outcome. Mandate 2038 is less exclusively corporate and more
institutionally plural. Its Trust, Systemic Risk, and four World Endings make
the shared outcome more central.

[New Angeles overview](https://drafts.fantasyflightgames.com/en/products/new-angeles/)

### Gaia Project / Terra Mystica — spatial economy

This is the closest spatial-economic reference. Asymmetric factions build
adjacent infrastructure on a shared board, where position, networks, upgrades,
and access constrain one another. In Mandate 2038, Teams move and Actions resolve on
the board; the districts are contested civilizational jurisdictions, not
literal geography.

[Gaia Project](https://www.feuerland-spiele.de/spiele/gaia-project/)

### Oath: Chronicles of Empire & Exile — political stakes

This is the closest reference for the game's political imagination. The
contest is not solely about who scored most, but about what order survives it.
Mandate 2038 has a fixed four-Era arc rather than an inter-game chronicle, but the
four World Endings give a match a comparable constitutional consequence.

[Oath's premise](https://ledergames.com/blogs/news/oath-chronicles-of-empire-exile-launches-on-kickstarter)

## Secondary references

- **Pax Pamir (Second Edition):** shifting alignments, legitimacy, and
  self-interest within a contested political order. Mandate 2038 has no tableau or
  card-market core.
- **John Company:** institutions as systems that no participant fully controls;
  negotiation is actual play rather than decoration. Mandate 2038 is more spatial and
  has a more bounded Era structure.
- **Sidereal Confluence:** joint ventures and negotiated economic
  interdependence. Mandate 2038 is not a trading game.
- **Dune / Dune: Imperium:** simultaneous commitments, uneasy bargains, and
  political competition. Mandate 2038 has neither combat nor worker placement as its
  core.

## What it is not

Mandate 2038 should not be presented as a 4X, a worker-placement game, a legacy
campaign, a classic Pax card-market game, or a conventional semi-cooperative
game.

## Mechanical identity

Its distinctive combination is:

> simultaneous action commitment + move on a jurisdictional map + asymmetric
> institutional engines + negotiated infrastructure + a separately resolved
> shared world outcome.

The game uses competitive institutional victory and civilizational outcome as
separate questions. A player can win the contest while the resulting world is
closed, plural, singular, or assured.
