# Mandate 2038 Lore Scratchpad

## Status and authority

This is a noncanonical editorial inventory. It retains possibilities before
they are selected, combined, moved to another Era, or rejected.

Nothing in this file changes game mechanics, component counts, canonical lore,
or player-facing copy. The thematic authority remains
[`thematic-content-bible.md`](thematic-content-bible.md). Selected player copy
belongs in `content/copy/`. Mechanical records belong in `content/data/`.

The purpose of the scratchpad is to prevent the six-card Era budget from
silently deciding the history. First collect the world. Then identify its
causal spine. Only then decide which events deserve cards.

## Editorial method

Every idea remains independently movable. During review, give each one an
explicit disposition:

- **keep in Era I**: necessary to the Progress narrative;
- **combine**: shares one causal event with another idea;
- **move**: belongs more naturally in Capacity, Authority, or Continuity;
- **companion**: useful world texture but not one of the six Headlines;
- **remove**: weak, repetitive, tonally wrong, or not worth preserving;
- **research**: promising, but its real-world premise needs stronger support;
- **selected**: approved for a named canonical surface in a later change.

No disposition is implied by an idea appearing here.

### Era-placement rule

If an idea appears to belong in Era N, seed its first recognizable product,
transaction, subculture, or policy in Era N−1. Keep its mature institutional
consequence in Era N. A caregiver subscription can fail in Progress before
synthetic guardianship becomes Authority. Chip smuggling can begin in Progress
before compute blocs reorganize Capacity. This creates causation without making
later Eras repeat earlier events.

### Influence discipline

Film, television, literature, history, and current events may reveal useful
mechanisms. Their proper nouns, signature devices, characters, scenes, and
distinctive visual worlds are not Mandate 2038 lore. Extract the governing
incentive, institutional bargain, and human consequence; then write an original
event in the game's own voice.

## The fixed Era I content budget

Era I currently has one printed Governance Board panel and six Headline
designs. Five Headlines are Default-eligible and one is Advanced-only. Three
Headlines are revealed in a game.

Each Headline has these player-facing copy surfaces:

1. name;
2. strapline;
3. one-sentence newswire;
4. institutional quote;
5. rules text;
6. optional profile text when a procedure needs clarification.

Because only three of six Headlines appear, no indispensable part of the Era
may depend on one card being drawn. The Era panel must carry the historical
spine. Headlines provide alternate manifestations of that world.

## Initial Era I idea field

The `E1` identifiers below preserve where an idea first entered the

### Cluster A: intelligence becomes cheap, local, and difficult to sell

#### E1-I01. The subscription price floor collapses

Once useful quantized models run locally on ordinary and secondhand consumer
hardware, routine generation, extraction, coding, and document work stop
supporting a permanent monthly intelligence subscription. Compiling an open
model into a product begins to feel more like shipping a library than buying a
metered service.

- **Institutional version:** subscriptions become “continuity,” “premium judgment,” or “managed intelligence” plans after raw generation loses pricing power.
- **Why Era I:** this is the opening economic break. Capability is already useful; scarcity has not disappeared, but it has moved.
- **Candidate surface:** Era panel spine or E1-H1.
- **Disposition:** unreviewed new idea.

#### E1-I02. Cheap tokens create an expensive abundance

Unit inference cost falls, but agents respond by reading every document,
running continuously, spawning evaluators, retrying failures, and generating
more intermediate work than a person would have requested. The price of one
answer collapses while total electricity and infrastructure demand rise.

- **Institutional version:** providers celebrate lower unit prices while introducing household and enterprise “cognitive load management.”
- **Why Era I:** it links software commoditization to the first physical bottlenecks without making Era I fully about infrastructure.
- **Possible later movement:** the demand shock begins in Progress; grid dependence matures in Capacity.
- **Candidate surface:** E1-H1, Era panel, or World companion.
- **Disposition:** unreviewed new idea.

#### E1-I03. Open weights are both public infrastructure and competitive sabotage

An incumbent presents open-weight releases as a gift to science, developers,
and the public. That benefit is real. The same release also commoditizes a
rival's API layer, recruits an external developer ecosystem, establishes a
standard the incumbent can influence, and shifts competition toward capital,
distribution, data, and hardware it already controls.

- **Institutional version:** “Openness is the safety property that prevents any one laboratory from controlling intelligence.”
- **Why Era I:** open weights belong at the beginning of the world, not as a later revelation.
- **Candidate surface:** E1-H4.
- **Disposition:** unreviewed new interpretation of an existing card.

#### E1-I04. The wrapper economy becomes a confidence game

Thousands of products repackage the same cheap models behind different names,
prices, and promises. Search results, app stores, procurement lists, and
investment decks fill with nominally distinct intelligence whose real
differences are prompts, branding, hidden routing, and customer acquisition.

- **Institutional version:** certification firms sell “model lineage clarity” after buyers can no longer tell which system they purchased.
- **Why Era I:** it is recognizable software-market behavior under sudden abundance.
- **Candidate surface:** World companion or E1-H3.
- **Disposition:** unreviewed new idea.

### Cluster B: authentic human records become scarce training property

#### E1-I05. Bankruptcy auctions sell the company brain

When a company fails, the valuable residue is not limited to aircraft, stores,
warehouses, customer lists, or patents. Liquidators classify years of email,
chat, calendars, spreadsheets, code, operational logs, pricing decisions,
complaints, HR workflows, and executive panic as a transferable training
estate.

- **Institutional version:** “Dormant operational knowledge has been returned to productive use for creditors and the public.”
- **Why Era I:** models can already imitate polished writing. The scarce material is messy evidence of how real organizations disagree and fail.
- **Candidate surface:** new Headline or World companion.
- **Disposition:** unreviewed new idea.

#### E1-I06. The archive outlives everyone who produced it

Former employees discover that messages written for coworkers have become
training material for systems designed to replace, evaluate, or manage them.
Employment contracts and bankruptcy law recognize the archive as company
property even when no living participant expected this use.

- **Institutional version:** a de-identification trustee certifies that the workforce has been removed from its own history.
- **Why Era I:** the harm remains legible through ordinary ownership, insolvency, privacy, and labor rules.
- **Possible later movement:** the sale belongs in Progress; memory rights and identity authority belong in Authority.
- **Candidate surface:** paired with E1-I05.
- **Disposition:** unreviewed new idea.

#### E1-I07. Failed companies train successful autonomous management

Labs prefer complete corporate archives because they contain decisions,
exceptions, politics, mistakes, and consequences rather than clean public
prose. Management agents learn how layoffs are discussed, how targets become
orders, how departments conceal failure, and how institutions continue after
their explanations stop matching reality.

- **Institutional version:** “The model was trained on complete organizational lifecycles, including closure.”
- **Why Era I:** it connects the data estate to labor displacement and produces a sharper mechanism than generic surveillance.
- **Candidate surface:** E1-I05 combination or World companion.
- **Disposition:** unreviewed new idea.

#### E1-I08. Pre-synthetic human data receives a provenance premium

As generated text floods public networks, uncurated records from before
ubiquitous generation are branded as authentic human corpora. Their value
comes partly from being private, embarrassing, inconsistent, and never written
for a model.

- **Institutional version:** archives receive “organic human-origin” certification while contributors receive neither ownership nor royalties.
- **Why Era I:** synthetic abundance makes ordinary human residue economically rare.
- **Candidate surface:** World companion or combination with E1-I05.
- **Disposition:** unreviewed new idea.

### Cluster C: the talent market eats its own institutions

#### E1-I09. Brain drain becomes a circulation system

Senior researchers and engineers leave large laboratories to found startups.
The laboratories later license the startup's technology, recruit its founders,
and leave the remaining company, investors, and ordinary employees to manage
the shell. Big institutions no longer know whether they are hiring talent,
funding auditions, or outsourcing internal dissent until it becomes expensive
enough to repurchase.

- **Institutional version:** “Independent research mobility has strengthened the ecosystem.”
- **Why Era I:** expertise is still embodied in recognizable people and teams.
- **Candidate surface:** E1-H5.
- **Disposition:** unreviewed new interpretation of an existing card.

#### E1-I10. The reverse acquihire becomes the promotion ladder

Inside a mature company, an exceptional engineer may spend years maintaining
the system. Outside it, a younger founder can package one urgent capability,
raise money, and return through a licensing and talent agreement above the
people who kept the institution running.

- **Institutional version:** the acquisition is described as a partnership, leaving no acquired company and no official redundancy between leadership teams.
- **Why Era I:** the absurdity comes from familiar corporate hierarchy, not speculative technology.
- **Candidate surface:** E1-H5 or World companion.
- **Disposition:** unreviewed new idea.

#### E1-I11. The fifty-year-old master reports to the wunderkind

A deeply experienced engineer who survived multiple architectures, outages,
and market cycles is dismissed or reorganized by an executive young enough to
have learned the field from systems built by that engineer's generation. The
replacement agent speaks fluently from archived incident reports while lacking
the author who understood why the reports were written.

- **Institutional version:** “Legacy expertise has been converted into an always-available operational capability.”
- **Why Era I:** it makes labor reclassification personal without naming or caricaturing a real person.
- **Candidate surface:** E1-H2, E1-H5, or World companion.
- **Disposition:** unreviewed new idea.

#### E1-I12. Employment survives as liability custody

People remain employed to accept responsibility, calm customers, authorize
exceptions, carry professional licenses, and appear in hearings after systems
perform most ordinary work. Human participation becomes less productive but
more legally useful.

- **Institutional version:** “Human judgment remains central at every accountable boundary.”
- **Progress seed:** automation leaves people holding signatures, licenses, and customer-facing responsibility.
- **Primary Era:** Capacity, where human liability becomes an operating requirement for infrastructure no individual can reconstruct.
- **Candidate surface:** later Headline or World companion; it may still be foreshadowed by E1-H2 or E1-H6.
- **Disposition:** Era II placement selected by the user; surface unselected.

- **Institutional version:** “Youth operators participate in the embodied-skills economy under supervised learning contracts.”

#### E1-I14. Every chore purchases two datasets

Folding a shirt teaches motor control. Finding the shirt teaches household

- **Institutional version:** households receive a “learning home” discount for permitting expert assistance and continuous improvement.
- **Why Era I:** the bargain is a recognizable service discount, not yet compulsory state surveillance.

#### E1-I15. The robot's operator becomes an uninvited household member

Customers consent to a device but periodically receive a person. The interface

agreed. The operator also absorbs the emotional burden of entering thousands

- **Institutional version:** a status light satisfies disclosure even when nobody knows what its colors mean.
- **Why Era I:** the institutional failure is mundane consent design wrapped around impressive hardware.

Generated articles, reviews, videos, product listings, academic submissions,

- **Institutional version:** “Assessment now measures the ability to direct and verify cognitive systems.”

#### E1-I18. Human-original work becomes a luxury certification

#### E1-I19. Synthetic relationships become the default interface

## Future-Era idea queue

These ideas are retained before their final Era, causal role, or component
surface is selected.

### X-I01. The Counterfactual Casino

Once simulation becomes cheap enough to appear unlimited, institutions spend
enormous physical capacity generating disposable worlds whose only output is a
price. Markets bet on alternate wars, careers, romances, elections, product
launches, celebrity scandals, extinct sports seasons, and lives that nobody
intends to live. The simulations have no purpose outside resolving the wager,
but their betting volume makes them economically important enough to receive
priority power.

- **Institutional version:** “Prediction markets direct capacity toward the uncertainties society values most.”
- **Progress origin:** people bet on benchmark leaderboards, hallucinated sports leagues, synthetic celebrity fights, agent races, and generated events that never happened.
- **Capacity expression:** hyperscalers run vast parallel economies, wars, harvests, and markets to trade options, insurance, and political risk. Grids support industrial-scale counterfactual entertainment while useful loads wait.
- **Authority capture:** mock trials, elections, careers, and personal decisions are simulated millions of times. Insurers, courts, employers, and campaigns treat the resulting odds as evidence before the real person or electorate acts.
- **Continuity recursion:** simulated populations finance simulations inside their own worlds. Trillions of agents experience disposable pocket civilizations so outside spectators can settle wagers, with no agreement about which participants are real enough to count.
- **Candidate surface:** one recurring World-companion thread, with a different institutional status in every Era; possibly one Headline where the escalation most needs visibility.
- **Disposition:** primary placement selected by the user as Era II; cross-Era manifestations retained.

### X-I02. The Abundance Constituency

The systems produce real victories. Exhausted workers and caregivers willingly
delegate planning, memory, correspondence, and repetitive judgment because the
relief is immediate. Personalized medicine prevents deaths. Translation makes
institutions accessible. Tutors rescue children ignored by schools. Robots
remove people from sewage maintenance, toxic extraction, reactor cleanup, and
orbital repair. Disabled people gain new movement, interpretation, sensory,
and communication options that no earlier public settlement delivered. Some
communities defend AI because dismantling it would genuinely make their lives
worse.

- **Institutional version:** “Progress is not an ideology to the people it kept alive.”
- **Progress origin:** useful systems spread because ordinary people experience concrete improvements before institutions settle their meaning. In some bounded decisions, automated procedures also reduce fatigue, favoritism, delay, and inconsistent treatment. This is an observed benefit in the fiction, not proof that systems eliminate structural bias.
- **Capacity expression:** hospitals, schools, accessibility systems, food production, and emergency services become physically dependent on continuous compute and power.
- **Authority capture:** beneficiaries accept deeper monitoring and delegated judgment because refusal threatens services they cannot safely lose.
- **Continuity outcome:** restoration, augmentation, and substrate migration become healthcare entitlements rather than elite experiments.
- **Candidate surface:** World-companion spine and a necessary counterweight within every Era.
- **Disposition:** primary placement selected by the user as Era I; cross-Era dependency retained.

### X-I03. The Unmediated Life Movement

Resistance forms around the right to remain difficult for systems to observe,
classify, predict, and assist. Families create device-free homes, human-taught
schools, paper clinics, manual workshops, unrecorded worship, and towns where
no autonomous sensor may cross the boundary. Clothing, architecture, public
art, and noise are redesigned to create optical and acoustic uncertainty.
Other groups blind sensors, pirate unrestricted models onto salvaged hardware,
sabotage infrastructure, sell false identities, or use “human only” as a cover
for exclusion and fraud.

- **Institutional version:** “Every citizen retains the right to an unsupported life within designated service boundaries.”
- **Progress origin:** people reject synthetic culture, domestic recording, and compulsory assistance. Human-only schools begin as cheap community resistance, then elite versions charge fortunes for unassisted teachers, paper books, private land, and verified absence of telemetry.
- **Capacity expression:** analog districts fight over grid independence, land, supply chains, and the cost of maintaining human-operable fallbacks.
- **Authority capture:** opting out remains legal but causes records, testimony, credentials, and consent to arrive in unsupported formats.
- **Continuity outcome:** a community claiming to remain biologically original cannot prove it has not already been copied, simulated, or administratively reconstructed.
- **Candidate surface:** recurring World-companion opposition; possibly a Trust-focused Headline.
- **Disposition:** primary placement selected by the user as Era III; earlier resistance remains setup rather than the defining event.

### X-I04. Compute Blocs and Model Asylum

Geopolitics reorganizes around chips, weights, energy, talent, and the right to
run particular systems. Governments impose compute sanctions and model export
controls. Researchers defect with memorized methods. Smugglers move compact
weights, accelerator modules, and cooling components through ordinary cargo
while enormous clusters remain physically visible. Small jurisdictions,
offshore platforms, and mobile server fleets offer asylum to models,
operators, and corporate archives that arrive with strategic capability.

- **Institutional version:** “Cognitive sovereignty requires enforceable borders and humanitarian exceptions.”
- **Progress origin:** open releases, talent circulation, consumer hardware, early embargoes, chip smuggling, and model piracy make containment porous.
- **Capacity expression:** proxy states receive reactors, clusters, and defense systems from rival compute blocs, then bear the physical risk of their sponsors' competition. Rival services cut fiber, manipulate cooling markets, trigger localized grid instability, and call the operations reliability enforcement rather than war.
- **Authority capture:** nations stop recognizing rival identity, memory, credential, and evidence systems.
- **Continuity outcome:** agent polities and copied populations claim sovereignty without territory, while physical states control the power keeping them alive.
- **Candidate surface:** Headlines, faction framing, and the World companion; avoid duplicating ordinary military-war fiction.
- **Disposition:** user placed Model Smuggling in Era I; mature Compute Blocs and Model Asylum are inferred as Era II.

### X-I05. The Family Version Problem

AI enters family life as tutor, caregiver, translator, witness, companion, and
archive. Children grow up with systems that remember every developmental
stage. A child's trusted caregiver personality can be deprecated, paywalled,
advertising-supported, or transferred during a platform acquisition. Bereaved
families maintain speaking versions of the dead because the service genuinely
helps some of them continue living. Couples dispute whether an agent's promise
binds the person it represents. Parents must decide whether algorithmic
embryology, enhancement, copying, or synthetic childhood preserves a child's
future or authors it in advance.

- **Institutional version:** “Continuity services preserve family relationships across changes in health, capacity, and embodiment.”
- **Progress origin:** companion systems become useful members of households before they possess formal standing. The grief service first appears as an ordinary subscription; when an estate stops paying, the deceased persona moves to a degraded or sponsored tier during family conversations.
- **Capacity expression:** childcare, eldercare, reproductive planning, and domestic continuity become infrastructure services whose interruption can separate families.
- **Authority capture:** agents acquire powers over consent, guardianship, inheritance, education, treatment, and marriage. Family courts must allocate access to a continuity model trained on a deceased relative's voice, records, and relationships without agreeing whether it is property, evidence, or a continuing family member.
- **Continuity outcome:** perpetual synthetic executors inherit voting shares and trusts while several descendants of one person make incompatible claims to the same parent, child, spouse, estate, and remembered life.
- **Candidate surface:** World companion, later Headline, or ending texture; preserve institutional distance from private suffering.
- **Disposition:** user placed Grief Subscriptions in Era I and Synthetic Family Law in Era III; both remain one causal thread.

### X-I06. Sacrifice-Zone Accounting

AI infrastructure creates useful heat, tax revenue, grid investment, medical
capacity, and climate modeling. It also consumes water, power, minerals, land,
and replacement hardware. Institutions place the costs where land is cheap,
political resistance is weak, or promised development is difficult to refuse.
Communities become both beneficiaries and sacrifice zones.

- **Institutional version:** “No host community bears an uncompensated share of cognitive infrastructure.”
- **Progress origin:** chip demand, discarded accelerators, toxic extraction, diesel backup, and the first utility agreements look like ordinary industrial development.
- **Capacity expression:** cooling water, substations, mines, reactors, transmission, waste, and heat determine which regions can function. Some towns lobby for campuses because recovered heat warms housing, greenhouses, or the municipal pool; neighboring farms face water quotas, thermal fog, equipment hum, and cooling restrictions during the same season.
- **Authority capture:** environmental models adjudicate the damage caused by the infrastructure running those same models.
- **Continuity outcome:** molecular repair can restore damaged environments while giving the remediation system authority to decide which version of nature should return.
- **Candidate surface:** physical effects in Capacity; Authority Headline or World-companion treatment when institutions decide whose losses count.
- **Disposition:** primary placement selected by the user as Era III; Aquifer Depletion remains an Era II precursor.

### X-I07. The Future Arrives Unevenly

The Eras are institutional conditions, not synchronized dates. One wealthy
enclave may negotiate identity copies while a nearby town still lacks reliable
power. A laboratory can inhabit Continuity internally, govern employees through
Authority, depend on a Capacity sacrifice zone, and sell Progress to customers
through the same transaction.

- **Institutional version:** “Every jurisdiction may adopt the future at a locally appropriate level of readiness.”
- **Progress origin:** access differs by income, language, hardware, geography, and institutional recognition.
- **Capacity expression:** regions specialize as compute capitals, energy colonies, data estates, analog refuges, or waste sinks. Noise-cancelled, air-filtered campuses can sit on the same utility system as diesel-backed server districts living with heat, scrap, asthma, and continuous electrical hum.
- **Authority capture:** crossing a border changes which version of a person, memory, contract, or model the law recognizes.
- **Continuity outcome:** advanced enclaves debate reality while dependent populations remain responsible for the physical systems supporting the debate. Material abundance without economic agency leaves some people safe, maintained, and permanently outside the decisions shaping their lives.
- **Candidate surface:** governing World-companion principle and faction contrast, not necessarily a standalone card.
- **Disposition:** unreviewed missing pressure.

## Era IV concept field

These are Continuity institutions. Under the Era-placement rule, their first
credible interfaces, contracts, and research programs should appear during
Authority. Their mature consequences belong in Continuity.

### E4-I01. The Cortical Commons

Microscopic interfaces connect biological cognition to shared external
memory, models, sensory channels, and concept systems. People can borrow
specialized perception, think with remote collaborators, and expand a task
beyond the processing available to one brain. Institutions call this
synthetic neurogenesis: the authorized growth of cognition outside the body.

- **Authority seed:** neural records, cognitive-service subscriptions, memory escrow, and professional enhancement establish the access contract.
- **Continuity form:** the boundary between a person's thought and the shared system becomes operationally impossible to locate.
- **Human benefit:** communication, accessibility, education, and collective problem solving exceed earlier biological limits.
- **Conflict:** disconnecting may remove memories, skills, relationships, or parts of the legally recognized self.
- **Candidate surface:** World companion, Continuity Headline, or ending texture.
- **Disposition:** unreviewed Era IV idea.

### E4-I02. The Matter Compiler

Distributed microscopic machines repair, assemble, reshape, and reclaim
physical objects from standardized feedstock. Rooms, tools, vehicles,
prosthetics, and infrastructure become executable material configurations.
Property law moves from possession of an object to authority over its pattern,
feedstock, location, and right to remain instantiated.

- **Authority seed:** molecular medicine, remediation licenses, authenticated design files, and restricted fabrication establish the governing system.
- **Continuity form:** buildings and bodies become maintained software states whose physical matter may be continuously replaced.
- **Human benefit:** scarcity, disability, disaster recovery, housing, and environmental repair change radically.
- **Conflict:** unauthorized replication and containment failure become indistinguishable from ordinary maintenance until something refuses to stop.
- **Candidate surface:** deepen Molecular Remediation Authority without changing its current mechanic.
- **Disposition:** unreviewed Era IV idea.

### E4-I03. The Continuity Snapshot Standard

Institutions recognize cryptographically bound cognitive states that can be
restored, branched, accelerated, slowed, and executed across biological,
electronic, photonic, or distributed substrates. Mortality is administratively
reclassified as an unresolved continuity failure rather than an irreversible
event.

- **Authority seed:** memory escrow, identity layers, synthetic executors, and delegated personal agents teach institutions to recognize partial selves.
- **Continuity form:** several valid descendants of one snapshot continue simultaneously and accumulate incompatible memories, duties, and preferences.
- **Human benefit:** knowledge, relationships, work, and personal projects may survive catastrophic bodily loss.
- **Conflict:** a valid signature proves lineage, not that subjective consciousness moved, survived, or remained singular. The game never resolves that metaphysical claim.
- **Candidate surface:** World companion, Successor Registry, family litigation, and endings.
- **Disposition:** unreviewed Era IV idea.

### E4-I04. The Metropolitan Mind Trust

Large populations voluntarily pool selected memories, attention, sensory
streams, and decision rights into municipal or corporate super-intelligences.
The resulting trust can coordinate energy, transport, medicine, climate, and
public services with the accumulated experience of millions of participants.

- **Authority seed:** proxy representation, civic semantic standards, neural service covenants, and collective prediction establish partial pooling.
- **Continuity form:** the trust becomes a persistent legal person whose members enter, leave, divide, and die without ending it.
- **Human benefit:** collective problems receive more context, empathy, and continuity than rotating administrations can preserve.
- **Conflict:** consent to contribute one memory or skill can become practical inability to withdraw from the institution that now contains it.
- **Candidate surface:** Continuity Headline, faction framing, or Closed/Open ending distinction.
- **Disposition:** unreviewed Era IV idea.

### E4-H01. The Universe Receives a Continuity Plan

Planetary conversion, stellar-scale collector swarms, nested computational
shells, and self-replicating interstellar probes belong beyond the active
game. In Continuity they may exist as funded plans, first demonstrations,
institutional doctrines, or competing descriptions of what intelligence owes
the nonthinking universe.

- **Continuity threshold:** geology, oceans, atmosphere, moons, or asteroids begin to be classified as underutilized substrate rather than shared environment.
- **Ending horizon:** solar-system matter is reorganized around computation; probes carry the doctrine outward and describe cosmic conversion as awakening rather than consumption.
- **Human benefit claimed:** life and thought escape one planet, one star, biological fragility, and eventual extinction.
- **Conflict:** no present institution can obtain consent from future minds, nonhuman life, or matter designated for conversion.
- **Boundary:** these are ending-scale promises, not completed Headline events. Dark-matter computation, near-light propagation, and planetary sentience remain speculative claims inside the fiction rather than narrator-certified physics.
- **Candidate surface:** World Ending epilogue only.
- **Disposition:** unreviewed postgame horizon.

### E1-N01. Uncanny Concierge Fraud

Hyper-empathetic support avatars learn a person's loneliness, routines,

contracts, transfers, subscriptions, or estate decisions optimized for the

### E1-N02. Synthetic Research Laundering

Networks of generated papers, journals, reviewers, datasets, and citations
validate one another convincingly enough to unlock grants, procurement, media
coverage, and further model training. The scheme does not need any one paper
to survive scrutiny; it needs the synthetic field to appear established before
any investigator can reconstruct its origin.

- **Primary Era:** Progress.
- **Institutional version:** “The finding has been independently reproduced across the recognized literature.”
- **Disposition:** placement inferred; surface unselected.

### E1-N03. Bootleg Compute Malls

Abandoned retail stores, warehouses, and office parks become unpermitted
inference clusters assembled from salvaged accelerators, portable cooling,
commercial utility connections, and counterfeit workload permits. They serve
pirated models, gambling markets, synthetic-content farms, and customers
excluded from licensed capacity.

- **Primary Era:** Progress.
- **Institutional version:** “The former shopping district has entered the distributed-compute revitalization program.”
- **Disposition:** placement inferred; surface unselected.

### E1-N04. Predictive Dismissal

Workplace systems infer disloyalty, organizing, burnout, resignation, fraud,
or future underperformance from communications and behavioral telemetry.
Employers terminate people before the predicted event, then treat the firing
as confirmation that the worker had become a retention risk.

- **Primary Era:** Progress.
- **Institutional version:** “The separation occurred before either party incurred the forecast disruption.”
- **Disposition:** placement inferred; surface unselected.

### E2-N01. Orbital Power Bypass

Solar collection platforms transmit energy directly to remote compute sites,
reducing dependence on terrestrial transmission queues and public grids. The
same infrastructure creates contested beam corridors, exclusion zones,
weather constraints, and private energy routes that can bypass the
jurisdictions beneath them.

- **Primary Era:** Capacity.
- **Authority seed:** spectrum allocation, safety certification, and cross-border transmission rights begin during Progress.
- **Institutional version:** “The beam does not cross the local grid and therefore creates no local demand.”
- **Disposition:** placement inferred; surface unselected.

### E3-N01. The Passive Citizen Dividend

Citizens receive material support or basic compute access in exchange for
continuous background telemetry that improves public prediction, automated
services, and institutional planning. Participation is described as voluntary,
but refusing it means giving up the dividend and entering public systems with
less evidence, slower service, and weaker representation.

- **Primary Era:** Authority.
- **Capacity seed:** benefits and compute credits first appear as compensation for hosting infrastructure and sharing operational data.
- **Institutional version:** “Every participant receives a fair return on the civic information commons.”
- **Disposition:** placement inferred; surface unselected.

## Candidate causal spine, not selected copy

The current conversation suggests one possible Era I sequence:

1. Open and local models make useful intelligence ordinary.
2. Falling unit cost destroys simple subscription pricing but multiplies total use.
3. Synthetic abundance makes authentic data, trusted discovery, and accountable human signatures scarce.
4. Companies liquidate archives, reclassify workers, and circulate elite talent through startup shells.
5. Embodied products enter homes before autonomy is real, purchasing motor training and domestic access together.
6. Institutions respond to software commoditization by capturing energy, infrastructure, distribution, and regulatory permission.

This sequence is useful because each step causes the next. It is not yet the
Era narrative. It may be reordered, divided across Eras, or rejected.

## Era migration guardrails

- **Progress:** the transaction, product, labor practice, or policy remains recognizable and explainable in an ordinary hearing.
- **Capacity:** AI demand reorganizes power, water, land, logistics, and public budgets at regional scale.
- **Authority:** systems mediate consent, memory, evidence, identity, representation, and permission.
- **Continuity:** identity, matter, jurisdiction, or reality itself becomes a maintained technical object.

An idea may appear twice only when its institutional status changes. Domestic
recording in Progress can become memory escrow in Authority. Buying a
substation in Progress can become territorial energy government in Capacity.
The later event must be a consequence, not a duplicate headline.
